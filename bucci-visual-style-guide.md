# Bucci Visual Style Guide

This guide defines the visual identity for all images produced by the content pipeline. It was derived from 10 published images across Substack articles, LinkedIn posts, and weekly roundups.

---

## Core Visual Identity

Every image is an **illustrated comic/manga-style artwork**. Never photography. Never stock art. Never minimalist corporate graphics. The visual brand is hand-drawn, bold, colorful, and character-driven — it stands out in a LinkedIn or Substack feed because it looks like nothing else there.

The images feel like panels from a comic book or frames from an anime — they have energy, personality, and narrative. Even a static image tells a story.

---

## The Bucci Character

A recurring illustrated version of Bucci appears across most images as a recognizable visual anchor.

**Consistent traits:**
- Dark, wavy/long hair (shoulder-length or slightly past)
- Warm skin tone
- Friendly, expressive face — usually smiling or looking determined
- Casual clothing: hoodies, grey t-shirts, sometimes a BJJ gi

**How he's depicted varies by article tone:**
- Energetic/action: In armor, wielding weapons, in a gi with a bat
- Reflective/personal: Casual clothes, contemplative pose, looking out at a scene
- Professional/business: Still casual, but surrounded by more structured environments
- Humorous: Brushing teeth, standing with absurd character mashups

The character is the visual thread that ties the brand together across topics. Readers should recognize "that's a Bucci article" from the thumbnail alone.

---

## Art Style Spectrum

The images aren't locked to one sub-style — they range across a spectrum depending on the article's mood. But they all live within the **illustrated/comic/manga** world.

### High Energy (action articles, frameworks, provocative takes)
- Neon color palettes (cyan, hot pink, electric blue, gold)
- Speed lines, explosions, shattering text/signs
- Dynamic action poses
- Maximalist composition — lots happening
- Comic book action effects (impact stars, motion blur)
- **Example:** The Elon Algorithm image — lightsabers slashing through "RULES" and "REQUIREMENTS" signs, SpaceX rocket, neon explosion

### Warm and Character-Driven (personal essays, roundups, team stories)
- Blue sky backgrounds, warm lighting
- Character ensemble shots (Bucci with pop culture mashup characters)
- Relaxed poses, friendly expressions
- Clean composition with clear focal point
- Bright, saturated but not neon colors
- **Example:** The Eclectic Resonance images — Bucci standing with Skrillex, a medieval knight, anime AI girl, and a surfer

### Contemplative/Emotional (reflective pieces, failure stories, personal growth)
- Nighttime or twilight settings
- Cherry blossoms, water, soft lighting
- Dreamier, more atmospheric
- Purple/pink/soft blue palettes
- Subtle visual metaphors (code floating in the sky, glitch hearts, lanterns)
- **Example:** The Haruru memorial image — nighttime cherry blossoms over water, code and hearts dissolving in the sky

### Conceptual/Editorial (business analysis, thought pieces)
- More muted, desaturated palettes
- Symbolic imagery (TV-headed corporate figures, AI robots in boardrooms)
- Can go darker, more cinematic
- Less character-focused, more concept-focused
- **Example:** Agent Corp — grey-toned boardroom with TV-headed suits, one colorful human in the center

### Playful/Explanatory (agent demos, how-it-works pieces, lighter takes)
- Comic panel layout with speech bubbles and sound effects
- Bright, kid-friendly energy
- Detailed scene-setting (playgrounds, everyday environments)
- Named characters interacting with AI agents
- **Example:** My Agent Will Call Your Agent — playground scene with kids and AI agents coordinating a playdate

### Minimal/Monochrome (habits, routines, grounded personal pieces)
- Black and white or very limited palette
- Simpler compositions
- Comic strip feel
- Sound effects and speech bubbles as visual humor
- **Example:** Productivity habit — B&W bathroom mirror scene, three people brushing teeth

## Recurring Visual Elements

These show up across multiple images and form the visual vocabulary of the brand:

**Japan/Tokyo imagery:** Mt. Fuji, cherry blossoms, torii gates, Tokyo skyline, bamboo, stone lanterns, kanji. This grounds the brand in Bucci's location and life.

**Pop culture character mashups:** Real or inspired characters from music (Skrillex/Daft Punk), anime, gaming (medieval knights, warriors), movies (Rocky). These mirror how his writing weaves in cultural references.

**Comic book language:** Speech bubbles, sound effects (SWING!, HIGH-FIVE!, BRUSH BRUSH), action lines, panel borders. Even single images borrow from sequential art conventions.

**AI/tech visual metaphors:** Anime-style AI girls with glowing blue eyes, floating code, wireframe hearts, glitch effects, robots. Technology is personified and made friendly/approachable, never cold or clinical.

**Blue sky:** The warm, default background for character-driven and upbeat images. Acts as a visual signature for the lighter pieces.

---

## Color Palettes by Mood

**Default/Upbeat:** Bright blue sky, warm skin tones, saturated clothing colors (olive green, grey, colorful prints)

**High Energy:** Neon cyan + hot pink + electric purple + gold on dark backgrounds. The "action movie poster" palette.

**Contemplative:** Purple night sky + soft pink cherry blossoms + warm lantern glow + dark water reflections

**Corporate/Conceptual:** Desaturated greys + muted browns + fluorescent office lighting + one pop of color (usually the human character)

**Minimal:** Black and white or near-monochrome with high contrast line work

## Image Prompt Guidelines for the Pipeline

When the content pipeline generates image prompts (Stages 5 and 7), they should follow these rules:

### Always Include
- Art style specification: "comic book / manga illustration style, hand-drawn aesthetic"
- The mood/energy level that matches the article tone
- Specific color palette direction from the palettes above
- Whether Bucci character should appear (and if so, describe his consistent traits)
- Cultural reference characters if relevant to the article (describe them, don't name copyrighted characters directly)
- Japan/Tokyo environmental elements when appropriate

### Always Avoid
- Photorealistic style
- Stock photo aesthetic
- Corporate clip art / infographic style
- Minimalist flat design (unless specifically monochrome mood)
- Generic AI art look (over-smoothed, uncanny valley faces, plastic skin)
- Dark/horror/disturbing imagery

### For Instagram Carousel Slides (Stage 7)
- Images designed for text overlay need:
  - Areas of lower visual complexity (solid color regions, gradients, sky, water) where text can sit
  - Consistent color palette across all slides in a carousel
  - Square format (1:1) or vertical (4:5) aspect ratio
  - The mood should match but not overpower — text is the hero on carousel slides

### Aspect Ratios
- Substack hero images: Landscape (16:9 or 3:2)
- LinkedIn posts: Square (1:1) or landscape (1.91:1)
- Instagram carousel: Square (1:1) or vertical (4:5)
- Instagram feed: Square (1:1)

---

## Style Evolution Notes

The visual style should evolve naturally over time as new images are produced and approved. The image style library in the content pipeline tracks which prompts and results were approved, building a growing reference set.

Key principle: **Consistency in character and art style, variety in mood and composition.** Every image should feel like it belongs to the same brand, but no two articles should look the same. The tone of the image should match the tone of the writing — energetic articles get action scenes, reflective articles get atmospheric scenes.
